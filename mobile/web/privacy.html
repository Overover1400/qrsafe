QRSafe — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

QRSafe is a QR code scanner and generator made by Semicolon. This page explains what the app collects, why, and how to have it deleted. We keep it short on purpose.

Who we are

Semicolon, Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. Contact: info@semicolon.live.

What stays on your phone

  • Camera. The scanner and the document scanner use the camera to read a code or photograph a page. The video is processed on the device. We never receive it, and no photo or video is uploaded to us.
  • Documents you scan. The images and the PDF are written to your own storage. They go nowhere else unless you share them yourself.
  • Codes you only scan. Reading a QR code made by somebody else sends nothing to our server.

What we store on our server

DataWhenWhy
An anonymous account id On first launch So the codes you make are yours and come back when you reopen the app
Your email address Only if you choose “Continue with Google” To recognise you on another phone and keep your codes
The codes you create — the type, what you typed into it (link, text, Wi-Fi name and password, contact details, email address) and the label you gave it When you save a code To list your codes again and to re-draw or re-export them
For a short link or a dynamic code: the short address and the destination URL When you create one To send a person who scans it to the right place
For a short link: the time of each scan, the browser user-agent string, and a one-way hash of the visitor IP address — not the address itself Each time someone opens your short link To count scans and block abuse

We do not ask for your name, phone number, location or contacts, and the app carries no advertising and no third-party analytics or tracking SDK.

Sign in with Google

If you tap “Continue with Google”, Google shows you its own consent screen and then tells us your email address and a Google account identifier. That is all we ask for and all we receive — we never see your Google password. Google’s handling of that step is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. You can keep using QRSafe without signing in.

Who else sees your data

Nobody. We do not sell, rent or share it, and we send it to no advertiser, data broker or analytics company. It sits on our own server, reachable only over an encrypted connection. We would disclose data only if a valid Canadian legal order required it, and we would tell you unless the order forbade it.

A short link is public

Anyone holding your short link can open it, exactly like any other web address. Do not put a destination behind one that you would not want a stranger to reach.

How long we keep it

Your account and your codes stay until you ask us to delete them. Scan records for short links are kept for 12 months and then removed.

Deleting your data

Delete a single code in the app and it is gone from our server too. To erase the whole account — the email address and every code with it — email info@semicolon.live from the address you signed in with, or from any address if you never signed in, and tell us what to delete. We do it within 30 days and confirm by email. You may also ask us for a copy of what we hold, or for a correction.

Children

QRSafe is not aimed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data. If a child’s data reached us, write to us and we will remove it.

Security

Traffic between the app and the server is encrypted with HTTPS. Access to the server is limited to named administrators using key-based authentication. No system is perfect, so if a breach ever affected your data we would tell you and the relevant authority as the law requires.

Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top and, for anything that materially affects you, say so in the app.

Your rights

Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA) gives you the right to know what we hold about you, to get a copy, to have mistakes corrected and to withdraw your consent. The contact address above is the way to use any of them. If you are unhappy with our answer you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
